The problem

Creator marketing is producing views, not revenue.

The same formats are running across every brand. Audiences are conditioned. Algorithms are exhausted. RoAS is inconsistent — and no one can explain why.

01 — Creative Fatigue
The same 3 formats run across every brand
Outfit transition with a beat drop. Try-on haul with trending audio. GRWM with a discount code at the end. Meta audiences have seen all three thousands of times. The thumb doesn't stop anymore. And creative fatigue hits in as little as 10–14 days — most brands don't have enough volume to survive the cycle.
10–14 day creative shelf life in active accounts
02 — The Measurement Trap
Hook rate is up. Revenue is flat.
The creative stops the thumb. Views look strong. But watch time falls off a cliff at 5–8 seconds — and that's where purchase intent lives. Brands celebrate view counts while hold rate bleeds out mid-reel. The CTA arrives at 25–30 seconds. By then 60–70% of cold audience viewers have already scrolled.
60–70% of cold audiences gone by second 15
03 — Creator Selection
Aesthetic fit is not audience fit
A creator's feed looks on-brand. The color palette matches. The styling feels right. But their audience may be 60% male, tier-3 cities, aged 18–22 — completely misaligned with your core buyer. No one filters by audience demographics, talking head performance, or product category depth. Aesthetic selection is costing RoAS.
Briefs are mood boards, not performance directions
04 — Zero Prediction
You find out what worked after you spent
Your best creative insight — which hook, which format, which creator archetype — arrives in the post-campaign report. By then the budget is gone. No one tells you what will win before you commit. No confidence intervals. No pre-spend RoAS forecast. Just variance you can't control and reports that arrive too late.
RoAS is reported, never predicted
